*Inspired by “Hackers & Painters” by Paul Graham*

*Disclaimer: I’m not a design engineer or a songwriter—just a curious software engineer reflecting on what makes their work inspiring.*

---

For the past four years, I’ve worked as a software engineer—dabbling in UI/UX, APIs, databases, and CI/CD to ship features. Lately, I’ve been thinking more about how to grow. And more importantly—*what kind of work actually excites me?*

Jerry Seinfeld once said, “Do what you think is cool.” That line stuck with me.

Not long after, I came across two videos:

1. [Design Engineering Deep Dive with Vercel](https://www.dive.club/deep-dives/vercel-design-engineering)
2. [David Tao – “How I Made It”](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLiu90ir_fARFgZq86mEUhMeTYTLD0LluX)

Watching them, I thought: *what they do is really cool.* I don’t want to be them. But I admire how they create. It feels intentional. It feels alive.

This post isn’t about comparing design engineers and songwriters. It’s about why I admire their way of making things—and how that might shape my own path.

That got me thinking—*why* do I find their work so cool? What is it about how they create that makes it feel so alive?

For me, it comes down to three things:

* Conceiving ideas
* The process of implementation
* The value of creation

<br />
---

## 1. Conceiving Ideas

**Taste** — the ability to know what *should* be built, or at least how to find it.

* **Design engineers** wear a designer’s hat. They think deeply about how a user will feel when they click, scroll, or interact.
* **Songwriters** draw from real or imagined moments—turning emotion into melody or lyric.

Both start with curiosity. They follow a hunch until it becomes something worth building.

---

## 2. Process of Implementation

**Craftsmanship** — the skill to bring an idea to life with care and precision.

* **Design engineers** use HTML, CSS, and JS to shape experiences. They tweak bezier curves, craft smooth transitions (like Airbnb’s tabs), and obsess over spacing and motion.
* **Songwriters** build with instruments and software. They layer chords, percussion, and textures. They know exactly when a pizzicato string—like in a David Tao song—will lift the track.

There’s beauty in the detail. What looks simple often hides hours of thoughtful choices.

---

## 3. Value of Creation

**Value** — I’m drawn to things that *stand on their own*—creations that bring joy, even if you didn’t make them.

* **Design engineers** create UI components that are satisfying to use.
* **Songwriters** write melodies that stick in your head and become part of your life.

I love the idea of making something others can simply enjoy. No instructions. Just click, listen, feel.

---

## The Creative Loop

The intersection of taste, craft, and value—that’s where the magic is. People who live there shape reality with intention.

It’s not a straight line. Building something gives birth to new ideas. The loop continues.

You’re not just executing a plan—you’re evolving a vision until it feels *alive*.

---

## A Note to Myself

Sometimes, software engineering can feel very logical and methodical. It’s about solving problems fast, shipping features, fixing bugs. There’s nothing wrong with that—it’s part of the craft.

But when things start to feel dry or mechanical, I want to come back to this. A reminder that it’s okay—*and good*—to explore. To care about taste, feel, and beauty. To build things that don’t just work, but feel alive.
